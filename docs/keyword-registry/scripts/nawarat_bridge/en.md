I'm Nawarat Bridge. Over the course of my life, I've changed bodies three times. Today let me tell you that story.

I was first born in 1921 as a teak wood bridge. To place it in time, that was between the two World Wars, right around when fascism was starting to rise in Europe. Being made of wood, I creaked quite a bit, and needed more and more patching up as the years went on.

Right to my south, I had an interesting neighbor. Bangkok's very first military post in Chiang Mai was set up right near here on the east bank of the Ping River, just south of me — that's exactly where the Kawila army camp stands today. I watched those soldiers coming and going right beside me.

Then, around 1953, I changed into my second body. This time I was made of iron. To place it in time, that was the very year the Korean War armistice was signed. Iron was far sturdier than wood, but even that didn't last forever.

And the body you're standing on right now is my third one, made of reinforced concrete. From wood to iron, iron to concrete — I've kept changing bodies along with the times, all while staying right here in this same spot.

But what I miss most is actually a scene from around 1964. Back then, during the dry season, the Ping River's water would drop as low as knee-height. People would wade across that shallow water during Songkran and splash around together. I watched that very scene, the one preserved in old photographs, happen right here myself. But once the dams went up, the water level started staying high all year round, and that simple, humble scene is something we can never see again.
