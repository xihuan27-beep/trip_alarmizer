I'm the chedi of Wat Ku Tao — this temple's stupa. Today let me tell you how I got my name.

As you can see, my body is shaped like round pieces stacked one on top of another. To people looking at me, it apparently looks just like a bunch of gourds piled up. So people started calling me "Ku Tao," which means "gourd stupa." Compared to the pointed towers at other temples, I'm a bit chubby and plain-looking. Still, I'm quite fond of my shape.

But don't underestimate me just because I look so humble. There's an old nine-volume record that contains a legend called the "World-Touring Buddha." In that story, I appear under the name "Weluwanaram." I'm counted as one of the eight temples prophesied to be the most sacred in the world. I might look round and unassuming on the outside, but I actually hold quite a distinguished status.

There's another story behind my appearance, too. Starting around 1856 — to place it in time, that was right around when the Crimean War had just ended with the Treaty of Paris — trade in teak wood with the British picked up, and Burmese, Shan, and Pa-O people began arriving in this area in large numbers. As they sponsored religious works here, a Burmese decorative style called Konbaung came flowing in along with them. Those distinctive decorations you can still see on my body today — those actually arrived around that same time.
