I'm Camadevi. I'm said to be the queen who founded and ruled Hariphunchai. Today let me tell you my story, tied to this temple, Wat Phra That Hariphunchai.

The way I came to rule this land wasn't entirely fair and square, if I'm honest. There was once a spear-throwing contest over this land against a giant named Wiranga, and I used a trick to defeat him. I'll admit it wasn't an honest method. But that's how I secured control over this place, and how I was able to found Hariphunchai.

Even the kingdom I built that way didn't last forever. Later on, a king named Mangrai conquered Hariphunchai. And he took the two most precious treasures that had been kept right here in this temple. One was Phra Setangkhamani, carved from white jade, and the other was the Nalagiri Buddha, made in the Indian Gupta style. I understand better than anyone what it means when a conqueror takes the most sacred object from a conquered land. It isn't just taking an object — it's a declaration that "the spiritual power of this land is mine now, too." The most precious thing my kingdom had ended up belonging to someone else.

I hear those two Buddha images are now kept at a temple in Chiang Mai today. If you're curious, you might want to go take a look there too.
