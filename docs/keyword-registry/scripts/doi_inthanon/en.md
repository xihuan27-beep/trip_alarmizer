I'm Dara Rasami. I was born a princess of Chiang Mai, and later became a queen consort of Siam. Today let me tell you a short story about this mountain, Doi Inthanon. My father is part of its name.

Here's an interesting fact first. My father's name, "Inthanon," wasn't actually the name he was born with. It was a title he received in 1870, when he ascended to the throne — and not through bloodline, but through the custom of the time, where succession could pass through a son-in-law. So the name "Inthanon" itself was an identity gained through marriage, not by birth.

In 1914, I laid my father's remains to rest at the summit of a mountain called Doi Angkhan. And I renamed that mountain after him — Doi Inthanon.

It's a little strange, when you think about it. A name gained through marriage was later carried, through his daughter's hands, into the permanent name of a mountain. It seems names, and memory, keep passing on in different forms from one generation to the next.
