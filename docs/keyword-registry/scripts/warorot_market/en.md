I'm Dara Rasami. I was born a princess of Chiang Mai and later became a queen of Siam. Today let me tell you the story of the lively market you're standing in right now, Warorot Market. Hidden beneath what you see today is a past that looks nothing like this.

Did you know that this ground, now so crowded with people, was once where the Lan Na royal family's graves lay? Right around here, on the west bank of the Ping River, royal remains were once kept.

But this spot flooded often. So between 1908 and 1909, I personally proposed relocating them. To place it in time, that was right around when Louis Blériot became the first person to fly an airplane across the English Channel. I planned and funded the whole project myself. It ended with a grand fifteen-day festival that cost 100,000 baht altogether. People called it a "modernization project."

Once the land was cleared, it gradually became something else entirely. It started as a temporary dawn market called Kad Luang, and over time it grew into the permanent market building you see today.

Other markets nearby share this history too. The neighboring Kat Ton Lamyai market suffered a major fire in 1968, and in 1989 the riverbank was filled in, narrowing the river to what it is now. The scene you see today, river and market together, is really the result of many layered events.

Looking back, it's quite ironic, isn't it? The ground the royal family chose as their eternal resting place is now the busiest, loudest spot in the whole city. I was the one who pushed for that change and called it "modernization," but honestly, back then I never imagined it would become this bustling a market.
