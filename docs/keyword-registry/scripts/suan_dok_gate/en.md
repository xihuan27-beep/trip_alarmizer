I'm Chao Noi Sukkasem, a nephew of the Chiang Mai royal family. Today let me tell you the story tied to this gate you're standing at, Suan Dok Gate.

Long before I was even born, this gate already played a special role. There was a pilgrimage route starting from old Wiang Chiang Mai, passing through this very gate, on to a temple called Wat Buppharam — what's now Wat Suan Dok — and continuing all the way up to Doi Suthep. That tradition is said to date back to the era of King Kuena. This gate was the starting point of that ancient pilgrimage.

But what I can never forget happened in 1903. To place it in time, that was the very year the Wright brothers achieved the first powered flight. I had to send away my Burmese lover, Ma Mia, for political reasons. And that farewell took place right here, at this western gate, exactly where you're standing now. It's told today almost like a legend, but it was something I lived through myself.

Then, a very long time later, in 2021, something interesting happened. Scholars researching the list of twenty people interred in the royal mausoleum, from King Kawila to Dara Rasami, found the name "Chao Uttarakan Koson" among them. That was me. I had long been treated as nothing more than a figure from legend, but it was only then, generations later, that scholars confirmed I was actually a recorded, real person.

By the way, that royal mausoleum where my remains once lay was later relocated by Dara Rasami herself. You can hear more about that relocation if you visit Warorot Market.
