I'm a silversmith working in the silver-crafting village near this temple. Today let me tell you a short story about this temple, Wat Sri Suphan.

Our silverwork doesn't allow for even the tiniest margin of error. Interestingly enough, the exact moment this temple's vihara, its main hall, began construction has been recorded with remarkable precision. It's said to have been in 1503, on New Year's Day of the year 865 in the Chulasakkarat calendar, a Thursday morning between 6:00 and 7:30. To place it in time, that was right around when Leonardo da Vinci was starting to paint the Mona Lisa. A record that precise, down to the hour and minute, somehow speaks to someone like me who does such detailed work for a living.

There's also another story behind how I came to do silverwork in this neighborhood. Later on, King Kawila carried out a policy of "gathering vegetables into baskets, gathering people into cities," and settled five ethnic groups here — the Tai Yuan, Tai Khoen, Tai Yong, Lawa, and Mon. The skilled hands of the people who settled here back then, generation after generation, are why this whole neighborhood is now known as a silver-crafting village. I'm one of the people who inherited that handed-down craft.
