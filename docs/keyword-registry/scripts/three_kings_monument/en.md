I'm King Mangrai. I'm the one who founded Chiang Mai. Today let me tell you about these two statues standing beside me, King Ngam Mueang of Phayao and King Phaya Ruang of Sukhothai, and why the three of us stand here together.

In 1296, the three of us jointly founded Chiang Mai's first temple, Wat Chiang Man. To place it in time, that was around when Marco Polo had just returned to Venice on the other side of the world. The founding inscription still kept at that temple has all three of our names carved into it, together, to this day. Our alliance wasn't just political. We called on the power of religion too, to protect and build up the new capital.

A very long time later, in 1986, something else happened. People opposed to a plan to build a cable car up Doi Suthep held a Buddhist ritual right in front of these Three Kings statues. They invoked the sacredness of this very spot to voice their opposition. I never imagined that, centuries after I passed away, the ground in front of my statue would become a stage for that kind of resistance. But thinking about it now, I have to say, it's rather a proud thing.

And since 2013, every April 11th and 12th, I get to witness something delightful. A ritual called "Honoring King Mangrai" was revived, and processions setting out from Chang Phueak Gate and from Tha Phae Gate walk their separate routes, only to all meet right here, in front of these Three Kings statues. The number of people joining the fingernail dance has grown too, from 150 in 2014 to 850 by 2019. Even more than seven hundred years later, people still come to visit me every single year like this — honestly, it warms my heart.
