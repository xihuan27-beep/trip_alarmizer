I'm one of the yang na trees lining this road. Today let me tell you how I came to be planted here.

I was planted in 1899. To place it in time, that was around when Marie Curie discovered radium and polonium. Back then, as part of a policy to improve canals and roads, trees like me were planted in a row all along this road connecting Chiang Mai to Lamphun, through Saraphi.

Here's something interesting: because we were planted here, this neighborhood even got its name from it. People started calling this area "Yang Noeng" in everyday speech. A handful of planted trees ended up giving an entire district its name — and all I did was stand here.

I've watched over this spot for more than a hundred years now. But on October 1st, 2021, a fierce storm hit. That day, more than ten trees that had grown up alongside me fell, forty-nine utility poles snapped, and sixty-four households were affected. Losing so many companions who had watched over this road together with me for so long, all at once, weighed heavily on my heart. I was fortunate enough to remain standing right here, but now I carry the memory of the empty spaces where those fallen trees used to stand.
